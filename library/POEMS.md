# Poems Worth Memorizing — Ranked Starting Library for Rote

Compiled 2026-10-27. Two inputs: (A) every poem Russ Roberts has quoted, recited or recommended on EconTalk, in *Wild Problems*, his "Poems of My Father" Medium series and his Substack; (B) the overlap across 15 "poems to learn by heart" lists (Ted Hughes *By Heart*, John Hollander *Committed to Memory*, Caroline Kennedy, Clive James *The Fire of Joy*, Poets.org, Penguin *Poems by Heart*, Leithauser/New Yorker, Robson *Heart Beats*, Inky Fool most-quoted-lines, LitHub, Interesting Literature, Art of Memory, NPR, forums).

## Scoring

Each poem gets 0–5 on four axes plus a Roberts bonus; max 23.

- **U — Conversational utility.** How often its idea applies to real situations (decisions, ambition, aging, grief, parenting, work, attention).
- **Q — Quotability.** Lines that land on their own and that listeners half-recognize.
- **D — Depth.** Does it reward re-reading; does it change how you see something.
- **M — Memorizability.** Length, meter, rhyme, structure. (Short rhymed = 5; long blank verse = 2.)
- **R — Roberts bonus.** 3 = he has it (or part of it) by heart / it's a signature poem; 2 = quoted from memory or wrote about it; 1 = endorsed on air.

PD = public domain in the US (first published before 1929). © = still in copyright: fine to memorize from a book or the linked page, but don't bundle the text in anything you distribute.

## Public-domain status of the top 25

Free to store, bundle and distribute (US): Ulysses, Stopping by Woods (1923), The Second Coming (1920), If—, Ozymandias, Invictus, The Road Not Taken, The World Is Too Much With Us, Fire and Ice, Nothing Gold Can Stay (1923), Milton's sonnet, Mending Wall, Macbeth, As Kingfishers Catch Fire (1918), Ecclesiastes (KJV), Sonnet 29, Death be not proud. That's 17 of 25.

In copyright: The Summer Day (Oliver, 1990), After Long Silence (Yeats, 1932 — US PD in 2028), Those Winter Sundays (1962), Piet Hein's grook (1966), One Art (1976), Do not go gentle (1951), The Secret Sits (1942). "Tell all the truth but tell it slant" was first published in 1945 and its status is murky; treat as ©.

Ready-to-import `.txt` files for the public-domain ones are in `poems/` next to this file (format: line 1 title, line 2 author, blank line, poem; blank lines are stanza breaks). They were typed from memory of standard editions — spot-check each against its linked source once in Rote's editor before you start learning it; a memorization app is the one place a stray comma matters.
